Version 3.6 works out what you actually burn from your own food log and weight trend, and shows the sum on screen. Your weight chart gains a smoothed trend line, goals are set as a percent of body weight a week, and each week the app can suggest a new calorie target that you accept, edit or skip. Logging gets a way to estimate a day you missed and to copy or move a single entry, and Analytics no longer colours over and under target as good and bad.

## What changed

- Measured expenditure: the Goals page shows what your last 3 weeks of logged food and your weight trend say you burn, beside the formula estimate, with the working written out. It needs 14 fully logged days and 3 weigh-ins, and it is on every plan
- Suggested targets: once expenditure is measured, the app can suggest a new calorie target with the sum shown, such as measured 2,350 minus 440 for losing 0.5% a week. Use it, edit it, or skip it until next week. Nothing changes on its own
- Trend weight: the weight chart draws a smoothed trend line under your weigh-ins, so daily water swings stop hiding your progress. The goal card shows your trend weight and its change over 7 days
- Goal rate as a percent of body weight a week, with a recommended band, instead of a fixed 500 calories. The form spells out the kilograms and calories it means
- Your maintenance calories: if you already know your number, enter it in Settings and it replaces the formula estimate
- Missed something? Estimate the calories you did not log, or mark the day as partly logged so it is left out of trend estimates
- Copy or move a single entry to another day or meal. Entries waiting to sync now show a marker
- Analytics shows over and under target in neutral colours, and the consistency score explains what it measures
- Fixes: a half-typed entry survives midnight, the slider handle sits on its track, entry names stay on one line, the Log sheet inputs are named for screen readers, and native sign-in links no longer leave a stray page in history
- Self-hosting: two new database migrations run automatically on startup

As always, everything here is in the open-source repository, and your data stays exportable.
